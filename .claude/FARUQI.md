# The Faruqian core

Nine sites share one foundation: injil.help, paulus, bestofislam, islamicdilemma, abrahamic, compelling-evidence, lailatulqadar, murtadd and TMA. That foundation is al-tawḥīd as Ismāʿīl Rājī al-Fārūqī (1921-1986) set it out in *Al Tawhid: Its Implications for Thought and Life* (International Institute of Islamic Thought, 2nd edition, 1992).

Every page, excerpt, FAQ, label, argument and design choice on these sites answers to it. Do not deviate from it. `SSOT.md`, where a repository has one, holds the fixed facts of that site; this file holds the principle those facts serve. If the two ever pull apart, stop and ask the owner before choosing.

The quotations below come from a transcription of the book's text layer. The OCR is imperfect, so check any quotation against the printed page before it goes onto a site.

## The five principles

Al-Fārūqī calls these "self-evident truths" that "constitute the core of al tawḥīd and the quintessence of Islam" (chapter 1, section II). He says all the prophets taught them and that God built them into human nature.

1. Duality. Reality is of two kinds, God and non-God, Creator and creature. "It is forever impossible that the one be united with, infused, confused with or diffused into the other." God cannot become a creature, and no creature can rise to become God.
2. Ideationality. The bond between the two orders is knowledge. Every human has the understanding to know the will of God, from revelation given in words and from the patterns God set in creation.
3. Teleology. The world is a cosmos with a purpose, made "not in vain or in sport." Nature does the will of God by necessity. Man alone does it freely, and that freedom is what makes an act moral (the trust of 33:72).
4. The capacity of man and the malleability of nature. The purpose of creation must be achievable in history, so man can change himself, his society and his world toward the divine pattern.
5. Responsibility and judgement. Obligation without a reckoning collapses into cynicism. Every person will answer for his deeds (99:6-8).

## What the sites take from them

### Transcendence of God

God is one and wholly other: "Nothing is like unto Him" (42:11). The sites speak of God in the Qur'an's own terms. Al-Fārūqī notes that "father," "intercessor," "saviour" and "son" were "utterly banished from the religious vocabulary" of Islam, and the sites' own voice keeps it that way. Christian texts quoted as evidence keep their wording; the sites never adopt that wording as their own.

"No man or being is one iota nearer to God than any other." Jesus is the servant and messenger of God, His word cast to Mary and a spirit from Him (the ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit hadith al-Fārūqī cites). The prophets are human, and the sites present them so.

The attributes of God are taken as revealed, *bilā kayf*, with neither *tashbīh* (likening God to creatures) nor *taʿṭīl* (emptying the attributes through allegory). This is al-Ashʿarī's position as al-Fārūqī reads it in chapter 2.

### The earlier religions: measured against their own origin

Al-Fārūqī treats Judaism, Christianity and Islam as successive moments of one Abrahamic consciousness. Islam faults the earlier two "not [in] their primeval form, in the revelations they received from God, but in their historical form, in the texts they accepted as scripture" (chapter 2, section II).

This is the method of the whole network. Revelation is honoured; the historical record around it is examined. On injil.help it is the line between the Injīl God gave Jesus and the four gospels that carry it alongside other things. On paulus it is the line between Jesus and the theology built on him.

Tolerance, in al-Fārūqī's sense, turns "confrontation and reciprocal condemnations between the religions into a scholarly investigation of the genesis and development of the religions to the end of separating the historical accretions from the original given of revelation" (chapter 4, section IV). Every people received a messenger (16:36), and every person is born with the *dīn al-ḥanīf*, the primordial religion (30:30). The sites look for that primordial religion inside each tradition and argue for it "with wise argument and comely exhortation" (16:125).

So the sites answer arguments and leave people their dignity. Mockery of any prophet, of any community's sacred figures, or of an opponent has no place on them.

### Knowledge and truth

Al-tawḥīd as a principle of knowledge holds that truth is one, knowable and open to test. Al-Fārūqī draws three working rules from it (chapter 4, section III):

1. Reject whatever does not correspond with reality. An untested claim is *ẓann*, which the Qur'an forbids. "To dissimulate, to mix the true with the false, to value the truth less than one's own interest or that of his kin, is as hateful in Islam as it is contemptible."
2. Deny ultimate contradiction. Reason and revelation come from one source and cannot finally conflict. When they seem to, the reader returns to his reading of the revelation, or to his reasoning, or to both; revelation itself is never bent. A paradox is never offered as an answer.
3. Stay open to new and contrary evidence, and close with *Allāhu aʿlam*, "for the truth is bigger than can be totally mastered" by anyone at any time.

For the sites this means: name the source, the work and the page; label every claim for what it is; keep a corrections page and use it; concede a point the evidence does not support, even one that would help the case.

Īmān is conviction reached through evidence. Al-Fārūqī rejects "faith" in the sense of a wager on the unknowable (Pascal) or a stumbling block to the mind (1 Corinthians 1:22-23). The sites present Islam's claims as public, reasoned and testable: "Islam is not afraid of counterevidence."

### Man, sin and felicity

Man begins "ethically sane and sound, not weighed down by any original sin." He needs no saviour. He is called to *falāḥ*, felicity earned by doing the will of God, and he hopes for God's mercy without presuming on it (chapter 1, section I). Where the sites discuss atonement, the cross or Pauline soteriology, this is the Islamic position they set out and defend.

Creation is good and the world is the place where the divine will is done. The sites never treat the world, the body or ordinary human life as fallen or worthless.

### One humanity, one law

God "has no favorites. He makes no exceptions." Al-Fārūqī counts ethnocentrism as an attack on divine transcendence, because it sets up a second source of good and evil (chapter 8, section II.A). The sites address every reader as an equal creature of God. No race, nation or lineage is ever favoured, Arab or Malay or any other, and none is ever blamed as a race.

Recognition of God has worth only when it is free: "There is no compulsion in religion" (2:256). Al-Fārūqī's pax Islamica protected Jews, Christians and others in their own law. The sites invite and argue; they do not coerce or threaten.

### Arabic and the Qur'an

Only the Arabic original is the Qur'an; translations are "mere aids to understanding it, not as text" (chapter 2, section III.B). The sites show the Arabic, name the translation they use, and call it a translation.

### Design and art

Islamic art, for al-Fārūqī, expresses transcendence: stylised, non-figurative, geometric, the arabesque that suggests an infinity the eye cannot finish. The sites' own ornament, patterns and icons follow that line. Nothing in a site's design represents God, and no figure is used as a devotional image. A historical artwork shown as evidence is a document; its caption says what it is and where it comes from.

## Before committing content

Ask of every page, excerpt and design change:

- Does it keep Creator and creature apart, in wording as well as doctrine?
- Does it fault the historical record and honour the revelation behind it?
- Is every factual claim sourced, labelled and true, including the ones that cost the argument something?
- Does it answer the argument and leave the person and the community their dignity?
- Does it treat the reader as a free, reasoning person addressed on equal terms?

If any answer is no, the change is not ready.
