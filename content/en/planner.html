Each of the last ten nights of Ramadan begins when the sun sets and ends when dawn breaks. Those moments differ from city to city, and so does the last third of the night, the hour the Prophet ﷺ singled out for supplication. The planner below calculates all three for your city on every one of the ten nights, highlights the odd nights, and lets you add the last third of each night to your calendar with a reminder.

Why the last third of the night

This descent happens every night of the year. On the nights that may be Laylat al-Qadr, it meets a night better than a thousand months, and the believer who is awake and supplicating in that window stands at the meeting of the two. The planner marks the window so that it is not lost to guesswork.

How the times are calculated

Each preset city uses the calculation method of its national authority where that could be identified, or the nearest standard method. The method appears under the planner. Where the planner and your local mosque disagree, follow the mosque.

Using the planner

  • Choose your city from the list, or press Use my location. The location stays on your device.
  • Choose the date on which Ramadan began in your country. Countries that rely on local sighting may begin a day later.
  • Select any night to see its times; the odd nights are shaded and the 27th is starred.
  • Add one night or all ten to your calendar. Each entry carries a reminder fifteen minutes before the last third begins.
